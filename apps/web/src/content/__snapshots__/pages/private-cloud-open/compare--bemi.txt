Logbird and Bemi
Bemi is a different kind of tool. It connects to your Postgres write-ahead log and records every row change automatically: the table, the primary key, the operation, and the row before and after. Its ORM packages attach application context, such as the user or the endpoint, to each change. You don't instrument call sites, and nothing is missed because someone forgot an audit() call. Logbird works the other way round: your code reports actions, including ones that never touch your database. [B1, B2, B3, B4]
A different category
Bemi is automatic database change data capture for Postgres. Logbird is an application audit log. Many teams could use both: Bemi as the record of every row change, and Logbird as the readable history of who did what.

When to choose Bemi
You want every row change in Postgres captured automatically, without touching hundreds of call sites.
You need before-and-after rows for every change, including ones made by scripts or migrations outside your app's code paths.
You want to query history from your ORM, next to your data.
When to choose Logbird
You want to record actions, not rows: a sign-in, a denied request, a failed payment, an export, or work done in a queue worker that touches no table.
Your data lives in more than one store, or not in Postgres.
You need the actor, the executor and the impersonator on each event, with operation trees across requests and jobs.
You want an MIT-licensed, self-hosted audit log with retention controls included.

What we couldn't verify
Left out of the table: Bemi's GitHub organization now redirects to pgstack-io (PgStack, from the same company, Bemi Technologies, Inc.), and pgstack.io lists a change-tracking product called "Audit" with different pricing. We found no announcement explaining the relationship, so this page describes Bemi as published on bemi.io and docs.bemi.io. Also unverified: which retention periods currently apply (the docs and the PgStack pricing page differ); supported PostgreSQL versions; whether a customer-facing viewer exists; licence files of the other ORM packages.
